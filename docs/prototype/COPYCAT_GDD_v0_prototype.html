1. Game Overview

COPYCAT is a short game-jam prototype in which the player cannot answer an exam honestly. To win, they must peek at a neighbor’s paper, memorize/copy a directional input sequence, and finish all 12 questions before time expires without reaching 100% Suspicion.

Core fantasy: “I am openly cheating, but I am trying to get away with it.”

The game combines three pressures: input execution, teacher timing, and resource/cooldown management. Cat-themed humor is used throughout the questions, UI copy, teacher reactions, and result messages.

2. Design Pillars

Cheating is the verbThe player must actively cheat to progress. Cheating is not flavor; it is the primary interaction.
Readable riskTeacher attention, Suspicion, danger overlays, wrong-key flashes, cooldowns, and the timer all communicate risk directly.
Comedy under pressureCat jokes and absurd distraction tools soften the tension without removing the mechanical stakes.

3. Objective, Win & Loss States

Primary objective

Copy all 12 answers before the exam timer reaches zero.

Win condition

  • Complete the directional sequence for all 12 questions.
  • The final result title is “Exam passed!”.

Loss conditions

  • Suspicion reaches 100% → result title: “CAUGHT 😾”.
  • Timer reaches 00:00 before all answers are copied → result title: “BELL RANG 🔔”.

4. Core Gameplay Loop

Read question→ Hold Shift to peek→ See neighbor combo→ Enter directions while peeking→ React to teacher→ Use Meow / Duck if needed→ Copy answer→ Next question

Each completed sequence immediately marks the answer as copied. After a short 0.7s transition, the next question begins.

6. Exam & Copying Mechanics

Peeking

  • Holding Shift reveals the active neighbor’s paper by sliding it into view.
  • Directional inputs are ignored unless Shift is currently held.
  • The required sequence is visible on the neighbor’s paper.
  • Correct inputs are marked as completed in green.

Successful answer

  • Completing the full sequence marks the answer as “COPIED ✓”.
  • Q12 resolves to “Answer: NO” as the joke answer to whether the player cheated.
  • Successful answers do not reduce Suspicion.

Incorrect direction

  • The current required arrow flashes red and shakes.
  • Suspicion increases by +9%.
  • If the teacher is already looking, an additional +8% is applied, for +17% total.
  • If the teacher is not looking, the wrong input immediately causes the teacher to turn around, unless the duck’s 5-second protection is active.

7. Teacher AI / Attention System

Default state

The teacher begins facing the blackboard. Random checks are scheduled throughout the exam.

Random checks

  • Teacher check frequency becomes more aggressive with each question.
  • Q1 uses a random delay of 2.5–4.0s.
  • Q12 uses a random delay of 1.5–2.0s.
  • Questions in between linearly interpolate both the minimum and maximum delay.

Watch duration

Every teacher look lasts a random 1.0–2.0 seconds, regardless of question number.

Teacher persistence while cheating

If the normal look duration ends while the player is still holding Shift, the teacher does not turn back. The teacher remains facing the player until the player stops cheating or a distraction overrides the state.

Almost caught tracking

Whenever a teacher look begins while Shift is already held, the game increments Times almost caught by 1. This value is used by the star-rating system.

8. Suspicion System

Suspicion is the primary fail meter. It is clamped from 0% to 100%.

EventSuspicion change
Cheating while teacher is looking+2% every 50ms ≈ +40%/second
Wrong direction+9%
Wrong direction while teacher is already looking+17% total
Successful Meow−12%
Successful answer0%
Rubber duck0%
Loss if Suspicion ≥ 100%

At the implemented rate, a player cheating continuously under direct observation moves from 0% to 100% Suspicion in approximately 2.5 seconds, before accounting for wrong-input penalties.

9. Meow Mechanic

Activation

A Meow can be triggered manually with M or by microphone input exceeding the loudness threshold.

Microphone threshold

  • The circular Meow UI visualizes microphone loudness from 0–100% as a green bottom-up fill.
  • The trigger threshold is 40% on this meter.
  • A red horizontal line indicates the true 40% threshold position.
  • Mic activation uses rising-edge detection: a loud sound triggers once when crossing the threshold.
  • The mic re-arms only after the sound falls below 75% of the threshold, preventing one sustained sound from repeatedly retriggering.

Effect

  • Immediately turns the teacher away.
  • Reduces Suspicion by 12%.
  • Applies a teacher distraction window of 2.5 seconds; a teacher check cannot begin while this distraction is active.

Escalating cooldown

The first Meow uses a 0.5s cooldown. Each successful Meow increases the next cooldown by 0.5s, up to a maximum of 5.0s.

10. Rubber Duck Mechanic

Activation

Use Q or click the duck HUD element.

Effect

  • Immediately forces the teacher to face away.
  • Prevents all teacher turns for 5.0 seconds.
  • This includes both random teacher checks and immediate turns caused by wrong directional inputs.
  • After the 5-second lockout ends, the next normal randomized teacher-check timer is scheduled.

Cooldown

The duck has a fixed 30-second cooldown. Attempting to use it during cooldown displays the remaining cooldown time.

Scoring consequence

Using even one rubber duck prevents a 3-star result. Duck usage is therefore a strong safety tool with an explicit mastery/scoring cost.

11. Exam Timer

  • Total exam time: 105 seconds (1:45).
  • The timer is always visible in the top HUD.
  • At 30 seconds remaining, the timer enters a danger/pulsing state.
  • At 0 seconds, the game ends immediately unless all 12 answers were already completed.
  • Remaining time is shown on the final results screen.

14. UI / Feedback Specification

HUD

  • Top left: game title.
  • Top center: answer progress (0/12 → 12/12).
  • Top right: exam timer.
  • Below teacher: horizontal Suspicion meter with percentage.
  • Desk right: Rubber Duck button with [Q] Throw the duck.
  • Desk upper right: circular Meow control with [M] Meow (or use mic).

Meow UI behavior

  • Green fill visualizes current mic loudness from bottom to top.
  • Red horizontal line marks the 40% loudness threshold.
  • An orange ring around the circle visualizes active Meow cooldown.
  • While on cooldown, the button and its hint use 50% opacity, while the cooldown ring remains fully visible.

Teacher danger overlay

  • Orange screen-edge danger overlay: teacher is looking at the player.
  • Red screen-edge danger overlay: teacher is looking while the player is actively cheating.
  • The overlay pulses and briefly flashes when a teacher check begins.

Notifications

Transient notifications appear on the left side of the screen so they do not overlap the teacher or teacher speech bubbles.

Tutorial

The start screen uses three horizontally arranged tutorial cards:

  • Cheat: explains Shift + WASD/arrows and shows example arrow/hotkey mappings.
  • Suspicion: explains the fail meter and shows a 60%-filled example meter.
  • Distract: explains Meow and Duck, including microphone/M input and the fact that both systems have cooldowns.

  5. Why is the red laser dot legally impossible to catch?
  6. Translate ‘pspspsps’ into formal Cat Latin.
  7. State Newton’s First Law of Cat Napping.
  8. What is the scientifically correct time for a 3 AM zoomie attack?
  9. Calculate the optimal nap-to-chaos ratio for one indoor cat.
  10. What is the primary purpose of an expensive cat bed?

The tone is deliberately playful, self-aware, and built around the contradiction between the exam’s “NO CHEATING” rule and the fact that cheating is the only way to progress.

Current pressure structure

The following is a design interpretation of the implemented values.

  • Early exam: wider teacher-check windows and short combos give the player time to learn the interaction.
  • Mid exam: combo length increases to 4–5 inputs while teacher checks become more frequent.
  • Late exam: teacher checks can occur every 1.5–2.0 seconds and the final combo reaches 6 inputs, increasing the need for short, deliberate cheating windows.
  • Meow: starts permissive, then becomes less spammable as its cooldown ramps.
  • Duck: is substantially stronger than Meow for preventing danger, but is limited by a 30-second cooldown and disqualifies a perfect 3-star run if used.

17. Rules & Balance Formulas

Suspicion while observed

Every 50 ms: if ShiftHeld && TeacherLooking && !Distracted: Suspicion += 2 Equivalent rate ≈ 40 Suspicion points / second

Meow cooldown

currentCooldown starts at 0.5s after each successful Meow: apply currentCooldown nextCooldown = min(5.0s, currentCooldown + 0.5s)

Microphone triggering

displayedMicLevel = min(100, RMS × 420) trigger threshold = 40% Trigger on crossing above threshold. Re-arm when RMS < threshold × 0.75.

Star rating

if Lose: 0 stars else if AlmostCaught == 0 and DucksUsed == 0: 3 stars else if AlmostCaught <= 2: 2 stars else: 1 star
